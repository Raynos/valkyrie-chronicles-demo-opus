# r25 audit — environment

**Verdict:** needs-work

## Summary
The foliage is the worst thing in the world and it is not a geometry problem — every leaf card is being shaded by the GRASS shader as if it were a single blade of grass, so each 1–2 m card prints its own dark-bottom/light-top ramp. Measured on a cold `closeup` plate, one leaf card runs L=123 across its top half and L=75 across its bottom half: a 1.64x luminance step inside one card, aligned to world-up, on every card in every canopy. That is precisely why canopies read as "dark green balloons/cabbages", and r24's Pass 4/5 attacked it with card COUNT and SIZE, which is the wrong lever — it removed density (making gaps, which then get sky-silhouette ink around each card) without touching the cause. Second worst: the same grass shader hard-codes `uPasture = 1`, so tree canopies are run through the sward's sage/olive clamp and come out the same pigment as the field. The ground is genuinely a flat tan band with high-frequency dry-brush and pepper noise but zero macro feature — no cobbles, no kerb, no readable rut — and the village is 13 detached farmhouses at a 13 m minimum centre spacing, i.e. not a street. Against that: the half-timbered frontage, the terracotta pantile roofs, the bridge ashlar and its three arches, the windmill, the Czech hedgehogs and the sandbag coursing are all genuinely good and must not be touched.

## Do not touch (verified good)
Do not touch these — they are the parts that already read as the right game:

1. **The half-timbered building** in `village` (right of centre) and the half-timbered farmhouse in `overview`. `buildFarmhouse` (structures.js:172) with its exposed frame, posts on bay lines, storey-break rail and shuttered mullioned windows is the single most Valkyria-looking object in the demo. It matches vc-100 and vc-092's right-hand building closely.

2. **The terracotta pantile roofs.** `hipRoof` + `PALETTE.tileA 0xb15c42` / `tileB 0xc4794f` (restored in r24 P2) read correctly warm and have visible courses in `overview` and `bridge`. This is one of the three things finish_plan named as recognition-critical and it is already done.

3. **The stone bridge** in `bridge`: three arches, spandrel coursing, ashlarMap at the right physical scale, voussoirs, coping stone-by-stone rather than a ruled ribbon. It is the best single frame in the set. Leave `buildBridge` and the ashlar decision (structures.js:1649-1653) alone.

4. **The windmill** in `bridge` — a strong, specific silhouette on the skyline, exactly the kind of thing the reference villages have.

5. **Sandbag coursing** (props.js:49-98). Cropped at 2.1x the courses, half-bag offset, inward batter and per-bag wobble all read correctly. The complaint "sandbags read as grey pebbles" is a *colour* symptom of the global desaturation, not a geometry fault — the geometry is right.

6. **Czech hedgehogs / dragon's teeth / crate stacks / telegraph poles with catenary** — all present, all reading, all correctly sparse.

7. **`bladeGeometry`'s arch and taper** (vegetation.js:107-157) and the r24 grass-density reduction to ~20 blades/m². Do not re-add blade density.

## Findings

### [blocker | small | verified] Every leaf card is shaded as a single blade of grass, which is what makes canopies read as balloons

**Location:** `src/render/materials.js:2550`

**Evidence:** Leaf cards run `makeGrassMaterial` (worldMaterials.js:1123 via makeFoliageMaterial). In that shader `hN = clamp(position.y / uBladeHeight, 0, 1)` (materials.js:319) is the fragment's height in its OWN local geometry. `lobeCard()` (vegetation.js:84) pivots the quad at its centre so local y spans -0.5..+0.5, and matLeaf passes `windHeight: 0.5` -> `bladeHeight: 0.5` (worldMaterials.js:1129). So for every leaf card: the entire BOTTOM half has hN=0 and the entire TOP half ramps 0->1. Two terms then fire off that: `albedo = mix(uRootColor, uTipColor, pow(hN,0.78))` (materials.js:2527) with rootColor=PALETTE.leafDark 0x414838 and tipColor=leaf 0x53692f, and `albedo *= mix(0.52, 1.0, smoothstep(0.0,0.45,hN))` (materials.js:2550) — a comment on that line says "blades darken sharply into the sward at the base", i.e. it was authored for grass and is being applied to tree foliage. Net: the bottom half of every card is flat leafDark at 52% brightness, the top half ramps to full leaf green. MEASURED on a cold `closeup` plate I rendered (node tools/shoot.mjs --cold closeup), on one single leaf card: top half rgb(122,127,104) L=123; bottom half rgb(79,76,61) L=75. A 1.64x luminance step inside one 1.5 m card, aligned to world-up, repeated on ~100 cards per tree. In a 1.7x crop of that canopy every blob is visibly a pale-topped, dark-bottomed ball with its own contour — exactly the 'cluster of dark green cabbages' read. vc-072's canopies have no per-cluster highlight at all; they are one mass whose value falls from the top of the CROWN to the bottom of the CROWN.

**Proposed fix:** Move the light-to-dark gradient from the card to the crown, and stop the grass base-darkening from reaching foliage.
(a) materials.js:2550 — gate the term: add a `uHeightShade` uniform (default 1.0, listed in applyNprOpts around :1993 and added to NPR_FORWARD in worldMaterials.js:915-928 as `heightShade`) and write `albedo *= mix( mix(1.0, 0.52, uHeightShade), 1.0, smoothstep(0.0,0.45,hN) );`. Grass/wheat/reed keep 1.0 and are byte-unchanged.
(b) vegetation.js:518 and :529 — set matLeaf/matBush `heightShade: 0.0`, `rootColor` equal to (or 0.90x of) `tipColor`, and drop `variation: 0.32/0.3` to ~0.10. The per-card hue/value scatter is the second separator: at 0.32 neighbouring cards differ by up to 44% in value.
(c) Put the value gradient back at CROWN scale, where the reference has it: in growTree (vegetation.js:346-395) record each card's normalised height inside the crown envelope, `ct = clamp01((py - (crownYc - crownRy)) / (2*crownRy))`, and in _buildTrees (vegetation.js:1182-1194) multiply it into the per-instance colour that is already being written — `c.r/g/b *= mix(0.62, 1.06, ct)`. instanceColor is already wired and already consumed (`albedo *= vColorC.rgb`, materials.js:2534-2536), so this costs no new plumbing.

**Acceptance test:** node tools/shoot.mjs --cold closeup /tmp/<you>-closeup.png and crop x=780..1400, y=60..540 at 1.7x. On any single leaf card, mean luminance of the top 18 px band and the bottom 18 px band must differ by <20 LSB (today: 123 vs 75 = 48). Then look: the canopy in `closeup` right-of-centre must read as one mass with a light top and dark underside, not as separately-shaded balls. Also render `grass` and `overview` cold and confirm the crown as a whole still falls dark-to-light bottom-to-top.

### [major | small | likely] Tree canopies are graded through the sward's pasture/sage clamp, so leaves are the same pigment as the grass

**Location:** `src/render/materials.js:2508`

**Evidence:** `makeGrassMaterial` hard-sets `uniforms.uPasture.value = 1` (materials.js:2508) before `applyNprOpts`, and the fragment then runs `albedo = vcPasture(albedo, ...)` and `vcSageFinish(albedo, uPasture)` (materials.js:2543-2546). vcPasture (shaderLib.js:365-378) pulls any hue inside 56-110 deg toward ~83 deg and applies the pasture chroma ceiling — it was written for the sward, and the comment at shaderLib.js:390 says so. Because leaf cards use the same factory, every tree canopy and hedge in the game is pulled into the pasture hue band and ceilinged there. Visible consequence in the `overview` and `grass` plates I rendered: canopy green and field green are indistinguishable in hue, and the canopies come out grey-sage. In vc-072 and vc-092 the tree masses are a distinctly deeper, cooler, LOWER-value green than the grass, and that value separation is most of how the reference reads depth in a landscape.

**Proposed fix:** `pasture` is already in NPR_FORWARD (worldMaterials.js:923) and already forwarded by makeFoliageMaterial via forwardNpr, and applyNprOpts (materials.js:1993) applies it AFTER line 2508 — so this is a one-line change per material with zero plumbing. Add `pasture: 0.30` to the matLeaf options block (vegetation.js:516-525) and to matBush (vegetation.js:527-534). Then re-author the leaf pigments at the freed chroma: PALETTE.leafOak 0x53692f / leafPoplar 0x6f785a / leafWillow 0x7e8566 (worldMaterials.js:134-136) should end up ~10-15% darker in value than the sward, not lighter. Re-author hue and chroma at the new luminance and measure there (the round-15 lesson).

**Acceptance test:** node tools/shoot.mjs --cold overview and node tools/artstats.mjs on it. `sat` must stay in 0.19-0.21 and `grn` must not rise above ~45. Then sample a 40x40 box in a canopy and a 40x40 box in open pasture in the same frame: canopy mean luminance must be at least 15 LSB BELOW the pasture's, and their hues must differ by >8 deg. Today they are the same pigment.

### [major | small | likely] r24 cut leaf-card count by 30% to fix a shading problem, leaving canopies you can see the sky through

**Location:** `src/world/vegetation.js:175`

**Evidence:** SPECIES cards are `[11,15]` (poplar :175), `[11,15]` (oak :182), `[10,14]` (willow :189). The comment at vegetation.js:361-368 records the reasoning: count was cut ~30% and size raised 18% because "a canopy read as a few dozen individually legible discs"; then Pass 5 reverted the SIZE change and kept the COUNT reduction. But the discs are legible because of the per-card two-tone shading (finding 1), not because there are too many — so the count cut removed density and bought nothing. In my cold `closeup` plate the near canopy is ~8 separated blobs with clear sky between them; in `grass` the mid-distance canopies are open enough that the bare bole shows through, which is the exact failure the module header at vegetation.js:165-170 says the lobe system exists to prevent ("a canopy you can see the sky through is a canopy you can see the branch structure through"). The gaps also generate their own ink: `skyEdge` at canvasRenderPipeline.js:838 is NOT gated by the object's outline weight (unlike `sil`, which is multiplied by lineW at :875), so every card silhouetted against sky gets a full horizon stroke — which is what draws the contour around each cabbage.

**Proposed fix:** Restore the count only, and only after finding 1 lands: vegetation.js:175/182/189 `cards: [11,15] -> [16,21]`, `[11,15] -> [16,21]`, `[10,14] -> [15,20]`. Do NOT touch `size` at vegetation.js:369 — enlarging cards was measured to backfire (the cluster texture becomes legible as individual cabbage-sized leaves) and that dead end is recorded in the file. Pass 4 already measured that a 30% card change moves centre detail by 0.6% (22.13 -> 22.00), so this is free on every metric; judge it by eye only.

**Acceptance test:** node tools/shoot.mjs --cold closeup and grass. In `closeup`, crop the canopy right of the hero: no sky must be visible through the interior of a crown, only past its outer silhouette. In `grass`, no tree may show its bole through its own canopy. artstats `detail` on the whole plate must not move more than ~1.0 (it will not; that metric is retired anyway). Frame time must stay under 16 ms — check the daemon's reported drawCalls/triangles do not jump more than ~25% (baseline for `village`: 275 draw calls, 1,627,472 triangles).

### [major | small | likely] The village is 13 detached farmhouses on lawn, not a street with two frontages

**Location:** `src/world/structures.js:1302`

**Evidence:** Plots are laid at `roadHalfWidth + 4.6 + rank*11.5` off the centreline (structures.js:1302), capped at 13 buildings (:1321), with a clash rule rejecting any candidate within `p.r + 8.2` m of a placed one (:1329). For a typical 9 m farmhouse, r ~= 5.1, so the minimum centre-to-centre spacing is 13.3 m against an 8-10 m frontage — i.e. a guaranteed 3-5 m gap between every pair of neighbours, on both ranks. In the `overview` plate I rendered, the village reads as four or five separate houses with lawn visible between and behind all of them. In vc-072 the street recedes between two CONTINUOUS frontages — gable, gable, gable, touching or nearly touching — and in vc-092 the buildings crowd the lane on both sides. Density is the recognition cue; the individual buildings here are already right.

**Proposed fix:** Three constants, no new geometry. (a) structures.js:1302 — bring the near rank in to `roadHalfWidth + 2.4` and the back rank to `+ 9.0`, so the frontage actually lines the street. (b) structures.js:1329 — drop the clash margin from `p.r + 8.2` to `p.r + 2.6`, and add a same-rank exception allowing gable-to-gable contact (`if (samerank && gap < 0.8) continue` only, i.e. permit touching). (c) structures.js:1321 — raise the cap 13 -> 20. Then re-check `_buildVillage`'s footprint registration still keeps vegetation out of the yards, and re-run the collider/nav sanity: buildings are `solid`, so tightening spacing narrows the AI's paths through the village.

**Acceptance test:** node tools/shoot.mjs --cold village and overview, side by side with docs/reference/vc-072.jpg and vc-092.jpg. In `village`, both left and right edges of the frame must be occupied by a building face at the frame edge — today the left half is open ground. In `overview` the village must read as a continuous row on the ridge, not four separate objects. Then play one turn (or run shoot.mjs all) and confirm no unit is stuck: 12/12 shots, 0 errors.

### [major | medium | verified] No fences anywhere in the village — the single cheapest missing recognition prop, and the template already exists

**Location:** `src/world/structures.js:1600`

**Evidence:** There is exactly one fence in the whole world: a two-rail post-and-rail run along the farm TRACK, `structures.js:1600-1635`. Nothing fences the village. In `village` the entire left half is open ground; in `closeup` and `overview` the yards are bare. Both the reference frames the plan cites have a fence as a large, foreground, immediately-readable element: vc-092's left-centre is a TALL POINTED PALISADE of vertical grey-brown stakes occupying roughly a sixth of the frame, and vc-096/vc-092 show low white palings and hedge masses along the yards. The existing loop at :1600-1635 is already exactly the right code shape — walk a polyline, drop `box()` posts into `bins.timber`, span rails between consecutive posts, push a `makeBox` collider with `cover: 0.35, hp: 25, tag: 'fence'`. finish_plan calls picket fences 'cheap'; that is confirmed — it is the same loop with vertical palings instead of horizontal rails and a different polyline source.

**Proposed fix:** Add `_buildVillageFences()` next to `_buildWalls` (structures.js:1560) and call it from the build list at structures.js:1187-1191. Two variants off one helper: (a) PICKET — walk the village-side edge of each building footprint (`this.footprints`, already populated) or the road corridor at `roadHalfWidth + 1.6`, post every 1.9 m, and between posts emit 7-9 vertical palings 0.08 x 1.0 x 0.04 at ~0.14 m pitch with a pointed or rounded top, coloured toward a chalky cream rather than PALETTE.timber (the reference's are limewashed). Two horizontal rails behind them at 0.35 and 0.85. (b) PALISADE — the same loop with 1.9 m stakes, no gaps, tops cut to a point with per-stake jitter, PALETTE.barnBoard/barnBoardDark alternating; place one run of 8-12 m on the village approach so it lands in the `village` shot's left half, which is currently empty. Collider: `cover: 0.5, blocksLos: true` for the palisade, `cover: 0.35, blocksLos: false` for the picket. Budget ~80 lines total.

**Acceptance test:** node tools/shoot.mjs --cold village. The left half of the frame — today a formless open ground plane — must carry a readable vertical-repeat fence element. Put the frame beside docs/reference/vc-092.jpg: the palisade must occupy a comparable share of the composition. Confirm collider count did not break pathing (shoot.mjs all -> 12/12, 0 errors) and that no fence post floats: every post's base must be at terrain.heightAt, checked by rendering `village` and inspecting the fence line at 2x.

### [major | large | verified] The village street is 20% of the frame with no macro feature — high-frequency dry-brush plus pepper noise, no cobbles

**Location:** `src/world/terrain.js:545`

**Evidence:** Cropped the `village` street surface (x 620-1140, y 590-850) at 2.2x from my own cold plate. What is there: a horizontally-streaked anisotropic dry-brush field (the `sward` term, materials.js:2920-2925) plus a uniform single-pixel dark speckle over the whole area. What is NOT there: any feature larger than ~3 px. No cobbles, no kerb, no doorstep, no readable cart rut, no puddle. terrain.js:545-557 does paint rut/gravel/dust variation into the vertex albedo, but the vertex grid is 0.625 m (terrain.js:573) so none of it survives as a shape at street-camera distance, and the near-field procedural fields are deliberately sub-metre. Mean colour rgb(134,119,82). The result is a large flat tan band that the eye slides off, which is exactly the 'the road is still a flat void' complaint the shader comment at materials.js:2899 quotes from earlier rounds. vc-072's street is unmistakably cobbled — individual pale stones with dark joints, receding — and it is the third thing (after half-timbering and pantiles) that names the game.

**Proposed fix:** Do not try to put cobbles in the terrain shader — the 4-layer splat has no free channel under VC_VCOL_ALBEDO and the vertex grid cannot carry a 0.2 m feature. Build the street as a separate ribbon mesh, which is cheaper and lower-risk: (1) textures.js — add `cobbleMap({seed})` next to `ashlarMap`, a ~256px tile of jittered rounded quads with dark joints and per-stone value scatter (pattern it directly on the existing ashlarMap, ~50 lines). (2) structures.js — add `_buildStreet()` called from :1187, walking `layout.road` for the samples inside `V.r`, emitting a triangle strip at `roadHalfWidth` either side, y = terrain.heightAt + 0.04, uv by arclength so the tile scale is ~1.6 m. Use `makeSurfaceMaterial({ surface: 'masonry', map: cobbleMap(), vertexColors: true })`. Taper the ribbon's outer 0.6 m to a wobbled edge (vertex-colour blend toward the terrain colour) so it does not end in a ruled line. Register footprints along it so grass does not grow through. (3) Watch for z-fighting: the road corridor is already flattened (terrain.js:193-233) so a 4 cm lift plus `polygonOffset` should hold; verify at grazing angle in `village`. Budget ~180 lines. This is the most expensive item in this list — do it after items 1-5.

**Acceptance test:** node tools/shoot.mjs --cold village and overview. In `village`, individual cobbles must be resolvable in the near half of the street and must mip cleanly (no shimmer, no moire) in the far half — check by rendering `village` twice cold and confirming byte-identity, and by eye at 2.2x on the crop x 620-1140 y 590-850. No z-fighting: no stippled tan/grey mixing anywhere along the ribbon edge. artstats on `village`: `sat` must stay <= 0.21 (cobbles are a pale grey-cream in the reference, they should pull it DOWN) and ink% must not rise above ~4 (today 9.29, which is already high for this shot).

### [minor | small | likely] The riverside landing stairs read as flat planked rafts floating half-submerged in the water

**Location:** `src/world/structures.js:1501`

**Evidence:** In my cold `bridge` plate, cropped x 400-1100 y 620-840 at 2x, there are two pale tan rectangles lying flat ON the river surface at the foot of the bridge piers, each subdivided into a regular grid of rectangles, clipping straight through the water plane with a hard edge and no waterline treatment. The two objects at the pier feet must read as a stepped stair descending into water — individual risers legible, submerged treads visibly darker than emergent ones — and must not present a single flat plane clipping the water surface.

### [minor | small | likely] Tree limbs are sub-pixel at any real distance, so trunks read as bare telegraph poles

**Location:** `src/world/vegetation.js:317`

**Evidence:** Primary limbs taper as `r = max(0.028, trunkR * (0.52 - 0.40*t) * rand(0.85,1.15))` (vegetation.js:317). For an oak (trunkR 0.32-0.50, vegetation.js:179) that is 0.17-0.26 m at the spring point falling to ~0.04 m at the tip; for a poplar (trunkR 0.17-0.26, :172) it is 0.09-0.14 m. At the ~35 m of the `overview` camera a 0.12 m limb is under 3 px and, once the ink pass's far recession (canvasRenderPipeline.js:920) halves it, effectively invisible. Only 3-6 limbs are grown for 7-8 lobes (`limbs: [3,5]`/`[4,6]`, vegetation.js:175/182). In the `overview` and `grass` plates I rendered, the trunks are perfectly straight bare cylinders from ground to canopy with no visible fork. vc-072's tree has a clear primary fork at roughly a third of its height and that fork is a large part of what makes it read as a tree rather than a lollipop.

**Proposed fix:** vegetation.js:317 — raise the base ratio from 0.52 to ~0.68 and slow the taper (`0.68 - 0.34*t`), so a primary limb starts at two thirds of the trunk's radius, which is what real limbs do. In vegetation.js:288 raise the limb count to `[5,7]` for oak and `[4,6]` for poplar so every lobe gets its own wood. Also bring the lowest spring point down: `t0 = rngRange(rng, 0.42, 0.94)` (:293) -> `[0.28, 0.92]`, so there is a visible fork below the crown. Cost is a handful of extra loft rings on five cached variants per species, i.e. negligible at runtime.

**Acceptance test:** node tools/shoot.mjs --cold overview and grass. Every tree whose full height is in frame must show at least one fork below its canopy that is legible at 1:1 (>= 3 px wide). Compare with docs/reference/vc-072.jpg's left tree. Confirm the trunk instanced meshes' triangle count has not grown more than ~30% (baseline `village`: 1,627,472 total triangles).
