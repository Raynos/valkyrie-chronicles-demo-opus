# r25 audit — characters

**Verdict:** blocker

## Summary
The worst thing is that Alicia — the hero of `closeup` and the face of the demo — is wearing a red-tinted garrison side cap and has essentially no hair, because `gearHead`'s scout branch (character.js:1506) never checks `o.bare`; her CAST entry sets `bare: true` (character.js:80) and it is dead code for her class. That single unchecked flag produces the "red leather bucket helmet", the missing ponytail and the bald forehead in one go. Second: her CAST entry sets both `collar` and `trim` to near-white cream (character.js:76-77), and rig.js:1995 documents in so many words that the collar's only job is to be "the dark ring that separates a pale face from a pale tunic" — so four fat cream tubes now ring her throat brighter than her cheek, which is the surgical collar. Third, and measured: the eye fissure is 33.4 mm wide × 14.1 mm tall (aspect 0.42); the same feature in vc-076 measures 61 × 60 source px (aspect 0.98). The eye is the right width and less than half the right height, so it can never read as a drawn eye no matter how good `eyeInk` is. I also falsified and then correctly diagnosed finish_plan.md's "the mesh only deforms cleanly over the procedural range": the skull displacement's clamp is never hit at any parameter value I probed (0.00% of 25,600 sampled directions), and the real cause of the "degenerate pale wedge" is a depth-buffer coincidence — the nose is a separate tube whose stand-off *and* radius both scale with `f.nose`, so at 0.62 two of its seven stations sit 0.6-0.7 mm *behind* the skin instead of 0.1-0.4 mm proud. That is a three-line fix that unblocks the whole anime-proportion pass. Genuinely good: the r24 grade is exactly on the reference (my own cold `closeup`: sat 0.202, centre/edge 2.71, ink 4.01% — identical to finish_plan's numbers), and the CAST colour work does land at squad distance.

## Do not touch (verified good)
Do not touch the grade or the drawing falloff. My own cold render of `closeup` measures sat 0.202 / centre-edge 2.71 / ink 4.01% / p1 44 / p99 229 — on or beside vc-088's 0.191 / 2.68 / 1.82% / 33 / 245, and the falloff-to-cream margin genuinely reads as the reference's paper edge. The r24 CAST colour work is also real and should be kept and extended, not redone: in `squad` at ~20 m Alicia (teal + white) and Rosie (red-orange + blue) are instantly told apart at a glance, and Rosie's blue collar arc + lapel tubes read convincingly as a knotted neckerchief — proof that the collar geometry is fine and only Alicia's colour override broke it. rig.js's `eyeInk`/`mouthInk` overlays in character.js:949+ and :1180+ are well-reasoned and already contain the catchlight and the sclera slivers; they are starved by the fissure size, not wrong. `buildHead`'s landmark canon (FY / T_* at rig.js:3305-3311) is a good abstraction — re-author its constants, do not delete it.

## Findings

### [blocker | medium | verified] Alicia's `bare: true` is dead code — the scout branch of gearHead never checks it, so the hero wears a red-tinted garrison cap and gets no haircut

**Location:** `src/actors/character.js:1506`

**Evidence:** `if (cls === 'scout') { ... return true; }` runs unconditionally. `o.bare` is read in exactly one place in the whole file — line 1644, `if ((cls === 'shock' || cls === 'lancer') && !o.bare)`. mission.js:130 rosters Alicia as `cls: 'scout'`, so character.js:212 `app.bare = !!cast.bare` has no effect on her at all. She therefore gets the garrison side cap built at :1521-1538 tinted with her CAST `cap: 0xb2342c` (:79), plus the cream welt tube (:1554-1569), the lit band seam (:1581-1598) and the two shell creases per side (:1605-1632). In my own cold `closeup` render that is exactly what is on screen: a maroon dome with a segment crease and a horizontal band, and a dark line down in front of the ear that reads as a chinstrap. Because gearHead returns true, buildHair (:326) takes the `coveredByHat` path, which builds only a nape tuft, two sideburn tubes and four 19 mm fringe locks (:382-399) — nothing that reads as hair at 200 px. Against docs/reference/vc-072.jpg, Alicia's hair mass is the *largest single element of her head silhouette*: a fringe covering the top of the forehead, two thick locks framing the face to the chest, and a ponytail as thick as her neck. In my frame there is no hair anywhere on her head.

**Proposed fix:** Gate the scout branch: `if (cls === 'scout' && !o.bare)`. Then add a headscarf builder for named leads — the reference scarf (vc-088.jpg, vc-072.jpg) is a flat frilled cloth square tied over the *back-top* of the crown with corners hanging behind, not a shell of the skull; build it as a two-sided card/lathe anchored at phi 0.20-0.45 on the *rear* hemisphere only so it never crosses the brow. With `bare` honoured, buildHair's bare-headed haircut path (:403-470) will run for `hairStyle: 'ponytail'`, and the ponytail cloth strip at :3181-3194 needs its section grown (currently 2 cols × 30 mm = 60 mm wide, 11 mm thick — a shoelace) to roughly head-width.

**Acceptance test:** `node tools/shoot.mjs --cold closeup /tmp/x.png` and crop the head at 2×. Pass requires: (a) no dome of `cap` colour above the brow line; (b) a hair-coloured mass occupying ≥30% of the head silhouette, including a fringe over the top of the forehead; (c) a red cloth element visible on the rear-upper crown, not a band around the head. Also re-render `squad` and confirm Rosie and Largo did not change (Rosie is `shock` + bare so she already skips :1644; Largo is `lancer` without `bare` and must keep his helmet).

### [blocker | small | verified] Alicia's collar and trim are both near-white, so the collar ring is brighter than her face — four cream tubes read as a surgical collar

**Location:** `src/actors/character.js:76`

**Evidence:** CAST sets `collar: 0xe4dcc4, trouser: 0xe4dcc4` (:76) and `trim: 0xe9e0c6` (:77). Those colours drive, at the throat: the collar arc (rig.js:2007-2012, tube section tx 0.025 × tz 0.019 on a 94×80 mm arc — i.e. a 50 mm-thick ring), the cream piping above it (rig.js:2014-2019), and the two lapel-point tubes (rig.js:2021-2027, rx 0.019-0.021 → 40 mm diameter sausages) — per side. rig.js:1993-2003 states the design intent explicitly: "A dark stand collar ... it is the dark ring that separates a pale face from a pale tunic, and without it a head is just the top of a sack." Alicia's override inverts that: in my cold `closeup` the collar mass is visibly *lighter* than her cheek. The proof that the geometry is fine is Rosie in the same `squad` render — identical construction, `collar: 0x35506b` blue (:101), and it reads unambiguously as a knotted neckerchief. In vc-072.jpg Alicia wears a *teal turtleneck*; the white is a panel down the front of the dress, well below the throat.

**Proposed fix:** Set Alicia `collar` to a value darker than skin — the reference's teal, e.g. `0x2f5f5c` (her existing `tunicShade`) — and move the white to the torso front. Keep `trouser: 0xe4dcc4` (the white dress reads correctly in `squad`). If a white dress collar is wanted, it belongs as the *piping* only (rig.js:2014), which is a 3.8 mm tube, not the 25 mm arc.

**Acceptance test:** Render `closeup` cold, sample mean luminance of a 20×20 px box on the collar ring and one on the lit cheek. Pass requires collar ≥ 25 LSB darker than cheek. Currently it is brighter. Visually: the head must sit on a dark ring, not float above a cream tube.

### [blocker | large | verified] The eye fissure is 0.42 as tall as it is wide; the reference draws 0.98 — the eye can never read as drawn at this aspect

**Location:** `src/actors/rig.js:3806`

**Evidence:** `const eW = 0.0142 * eyeS, eH = 0.0060 * eyeS;` (duplicated deliberately at character.js:958 for the `eyeInk` overlay). For Alicia, `f.eye = 1.14` → `eyeS = 1.175`, giving a fissure of 33.4 × 14.1 mm. I measured the built head from the module itself (calling the exported `buildHead` with a stub builder): face height 198.1 mm, face width 120.8 mm. So the eye is 27.6% of face width and 11.7% of face width tall. Ground truth, measured at 6× on docs/reference/vc-076.jpg (crop origin 1180,420): the dark almond spans 61 source px wide × 60 source px tall on a 230 px face — 26.5% of face width and 26% tall. Width matches; height is 2.2× short. The iris is worse: ours is 14.6 mm on a 33.4 mm fissure (44% of fissure width); the reference iris measures 50 of 61 px (82%), with the sclera reduced to two thin crescents. `eyeInk` (character.js:1036-1046) then deliberately draws an *even smaller* iris over rig's, `radius: [0.0075*eyeS, ...]`, on the round-17 theory that the eye should be "a third iris, two thirds white" — that theory is contradicted by the reference frame.

**Proposed fix:** This is the real mesh-level work. (1) `eH` 0.0060 → ~0.0135 in both rig.js:3806 and character.js:958. (2) `eyeInk`'s iris to ~0.82 of fissure width and the sclera slivers back to corner crescents. (3) The socket must grow with it: rig.js:3439 `blob(dy - (eyeY+0.035), 0.155, ax-0.42, 0.235)` is cut for a 13 mm fissure. (4) The blocker behind that is the landmark canon at rig.js:3309-3311 — `T_EYE = 0.500, T_BROW = 0.545` puts the brow only 0.045 of face height (≈9 mm) above the eye line, which is the classical human canon. A 31 mm-tall eye does not fit in 9 mm of clearance. The anime canon drops the eye line to ≈0.44 and lifts the brow to ≈0.60. Re-author those constants for the cast (they are already threaded through every landmark, so the rest follows), then re-tune the socket, brow ridge (:3433) and zygomatic (:3512).

**Acceptance test:** Render `closeup` cold, crop the hero's near eye at 6×, measure the bounding box of the dark almond (lash margin to lower lid). Pass requires height/width ≥ 0.85 and width ≥ 0.24 of the face's skin width. Then render `overview` and confirm the eye still reads as one dark mark at 30 px rather than two — the round-2 failure mode.

### [major | small | verified] The 'degenerate pale wedge' that blocked P6a is a sub-millimetre depth coincidence in the nose tube, not a mesh deformation limit — and it is a three-line fix

**Location:** `src/actors/rig.js:3735`

**Evidence:** finish_plan.md:498-501 records this as a hard blocker ("the parameters are already at the edge of the range the mesh deforms cleanly over, proved by breaking it once"). I probed the actual mesh by importing the exported `buildHead` with a stub builder and reading back the returned `disp` and the captured nose tube stations. First, the stated cause is wrong: the displacement's clamp at rig.js:3693 `clamp(sx,0.45,1.45)` is hit by 0.00% of 25,600 sampled directions at every parameter set I tried, including nose 0.62 / jaw 0.24 and the procedural extremes — so nothing is deforming degenerately. The real cause is here: `const pj = 0.0118 * f.nose` scales BOTH the tube's stand-off from the skin AND its radius, so lowering `f.nose` walks the tube backwards into the skull. Measured front-face protrusion beyond the skull skin, per station: at f.nose 0.80 (the shipped floor) stations 1 (nasion) and 6 (alar base) stand +0.4 mm and +0.1 mm proud; at f.nose 0.62 the same stations are −0.7 mm and −0.6 mm, i.e. *inside* the head, and station 4's back face is 0.1 mm from the skin. Two coincident surfaces at that separation z-fight, and the nose material is `mixCol(o.skin, [0.92,0.90,0.88], 0.05)` (rig.js:3736) — brighter than skin. A z-fighting pale surface across the middle of the face is exactly "a degenerate pale wedge". The shipped 0.80 is only 0.1 mm from the same failure.

**Proposed fix:** Apply the pattern gearHead already uses for exactly this hazard (`const MINK = 1.045` at character.js:1458, with its own comment about the depth buffer eating shells that sink inside the skull). Clamp every nose station so its front face stands a fixed margin proud of the skin: compute `sz = surf(0, dyOfStation, front)[2]` and force `p[2] + rz >= sz + 0.003`. Do the same for the alae (rig.js:3749-3755) and the nostril discs (:3760-3766). Then `f.nose` is free down to ~0.35 and the anime nose becomes a parameter change.

**Acceptance test:** Reproduce the probe: a scratch ESM that imports `buildHead` from src/actors/rig.js, passes a stub `b` that records `addTube` station arrays, and a stub rig `{restWorld:{head:{pos:{x:0,y:1.549,z:0.006}}},proportions:{head:1}}`; then for each nose station solve for the midline skin z at that station's y using the returned `disp`. Pass requires protrusion ≥ 3 mm at every station for `f.nose` swept over [0.35, 1.25]. Then set Alicia's `face.nose` to 0.45 in the CAST table and render `closeup` cold — the nose must be a small soft wedge with no pale artefact anywhere on the face.

### [major | medium | verified] The face carries ~20 albedo marks of comparable weight; the reference carries five — and the 4-band quantiser hardens the soft ones into a shape that reads as a surgical mask

**Location:** `src/actors/rig.js:4141`

**Evidence:** The `faceMap` closure at rig.js:4141-4338 lays, in one pass: eye slot, orbital socket, inner canthus, mouth lozenge, commissure, mental shadow, lower-lip light, nostril, brow bar, brow ridge light, hairline, ear whorl, temple, perioral chain, buccal hollow, subnasal, forehead light, crown, occiput shelf, under-jaw wedge, cheekbone division, side-plane falloff and a three-lobe blush. Several are broad and soft (the 0.215 socket at half-width 0.150×0.240, the 0.090 temple at 0.26×0.220, the 0.062 buccal). config.js:115 `bands: 4` then quantises the shading into four levels, which turns those soft gradients into hard-edged plateaus. In my own cold `closeup` at 5× the result is a single mid-dark amoeba with a hard bright rim running from the ear across the cheek to the nose — it reads as a surgical mask over the nose and mouth. The same shape appears on the foreground shocktrooper in my cold `village` render under completely different lighting, so it is albedo + quantiser, not a cast shadow. Against vc-076.jpg the reference face has *five* marks and nothing else: two lash-and-iris eyes, two brow strokes, one 4 mm nose shadow, one short mouth line. No jaw line, no nasolabial, no ear whorl, no cheek wash. rig.js:4118-4128 itself records that hard albedo steps read by the quantiser as wash boundaries is the documented trap — the pass declined to add *new* hard steps but the existing soft ones are being hardened anyway.

**Proposed fix:** For the named cast, take a different branch of `faceMap` that keeps only: eye slot, brow bar, nostril, mouth lozenge, under-jaw wedge — and drops temple, perioral chain, buccal hollow, ear whorl, cheekbone division, subnasal, mental shadow and the blush lobes. This is a subtractive change and cheap to trial. Do not chase it with a metric.

**Acceptance test:** Render `closeup` cold and crop the face at 3×. Count distinct dark marks enclosed by the face silhouette (excluding the outline pass). Pass requires ≤ 6, and specifically requires that no single connected dark region spans more than ~35% of the visible face area. Cross-check `village` at the same time — the foreground soldier's mask shape must be gone there too.

### [major | medium | verified] Hands are five individually modelled sausage fingers with heavy interior ink between each; the reference draws a mitt

**Location:** `src/actors/rig.js:2652`

**Evidence:** `buildHands` builds a rounded-box palm (:2679, 30×84×80 mm), a thenar pad, a hypothenar, four separate metacarpal knuckle heads, and then per-finger phalanx geometry. In my own cold `closeup` crop at 3× of the two hands on the rifle stock, each finger is a separate pale sausage with a full-weight dark contour down both sides, splayed over the stock with visible gaps rather than gripping it — the hands are the second-highest-contrast object in the frame after the character outline, and they read as spider legs. In docs/reference/vc-072.jpg every hand in frame (Alicia's, Welkin's, Isara's) is a simple soft mitt with fingerless gloves and *no* interior line between fingers at all; the whole hand carries one contour.

**Proposed fix:** Either simplify the hand at all LODs to a mitt with a thumb and one grouped finger mass, or — cheaper and reversible — gate the crease-ink term on the hand zone so the interior finger separations stop drawing, leaving one silhouette contour. The second is a shader/zone change and does not touch the mesh.

**Acceptance test:** Render `closeup` cold and crop the trigger hand at 3×. Pass requires ≤1 interior dark line inside the hand silhouette, and the hand must read as one mass gripping the stock. Re-render `aim` to confirm the firing grip did not become a paddle.

### [minor | medium | verified] The shoulders read as two round pauldrons plus a chest slab — nobody in the reference wears anything on their shoulders

**Location:** `src/actors/rig.js:2184`

**Evidence:** Two masses stack at the shoulder: the deltoid cap ellipsoid at rig.js:2182-2186 (`radius: [0.099*g, 0.053*g, 0.090*g]` — a 198×106×180 mm lump, deliberately pushed to be the outermost geometry per the round-15 note at :2160-2181), and for shocktroopers the chest rig tube at character.js:1819-1823 (`rx 0.108-0.116*g` → a 216-232 mm wide, ~105 mm deep barrel across the chest) with four `PALETTE.metalDark` magazine boxes on it (:1825-1829). In my own cold `village` render the foreground shocktrooper's shoulders are two discrete rounded lumps with a dark navy slab between them — the four magazine boxes are individually legible on it. In vc-072.jpg and vc-108.jpg no Gallian militia figure has any shoulder or chest mass at all; the silhouette is a slim column, and kit is a satchel slung *low* on one hip.

**Proposed fix:** Delete or greatly shrink the shock chest rig (character.js:1818-1831) — it is an invented WW2-prior element — and flatten the deltoid cap's `dy` and lateral gain so the shoulder is one continuous convex curve from acromion into sleeve rather than a distinct ball. Note rig.js:2160-2181 records that pulling the cap *inboard* was tried in round 10 and produced a dead-straight ink edge; the fix is to flatten it in section, not to move it in.

**Acceptance test:** Render `village` and `squad` cold. Pass requires the shoulder-to-upper-arm outline to be a single unbroken convex curve with no separate lump boundary, and no dark chest slab with legible boxes on it. Check the round-15 regression at the same time: no straight vertical ink edge down the outer arm.
